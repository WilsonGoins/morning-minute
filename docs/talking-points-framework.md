# Talking Points Framework

Talking points are generated using a structured framework for analyzing financial news in a morning meeting context. Each article produces exactly 7 talking points, one per framework prompt.

---

## The Seven Prompts

### 1. Expected vs. Happened
What did the market or analysts expect, and how did the actual result differ? This is the most important frame — markets move on surprises, not outcomes.

*Example: "Consensus expected a 25bps cut. The Fed held rates flat, citing persistent services inflation. This was a hawkish surprise."*

### 2. Price Reaction
How did relevant assets move in response to the news? Reference specific instruments, direction, and magnitude where possible.

*Example: "2yr Treasury yields rose 8bps on the decision. The dollar strengthened 0.4% against a basket of peers. Equities sold off modestly — S&P futures down 0.3%."*

### 3. The Flows
How did people trade? What positions were opened, closed, or rotated into? This prompt looks beyond price to the behavior driving it.

*Example: "Rate-sensitive sectors (utilities, REITs) saw heavy selling. Money market funds saw inflows as investors extended cash holdings. Options markets saw a surge in put buying on the belly of the curve."*

### 4. Change in Expectations
How does this news shift what the market expects next — for policy, data, earnings, or macro outcomes? Look at futures pricing, forward rates, or analyst revisions.

*Example: "Fed funds futures now price only one cut in 2025, down from two. The first cut is now fully priced for December, pushed back from September."*

### 5. Cross-Asset Links
How does this connect to other asset classes? Financial markets are interconnected — a bond move affects equities, a dollar move affects commodities, a China data miss affects emerging markets.

*Example: "A stronger dollar is putting pressure on gold, which fell 0.8%. Oil is also softer as a stronger dollar makes dollar-denominated commodities more expensive for foreign buyers."*

### 6. Fact or Speculation
Is the news confirmed data, a policy decision, or analyst opinion/rumor? Distinguishing between these matters — markets can over-react to speculation and under-react to data.

*Example: "This is a confirmed policy decision from the FOMC — not a leak or a speech. The statement and the dot plot are the authoritative sources."*

### 7. Historical Comparison
How do today's moves or outcomes compare to similar past events? Context from history helps calibrate whether the reaction is proportionate.

*Example: "The last time the Fed surprised with a hold at a meeting where a cut was priced was December 2023. The 2yr yield sold off 12bps that day — today's 8bps move is milder, suggesting the market is less caught off guard."*

---

## Generation Details

Talking points are generated by `claude-sonnet-4-6` via `lib/anthropic.ts → generateTalkingPoints()`. The prompt instructs Claude to produce exactly 7 items in the order above, each 2–4 sentences long.

For paywalled articles (FT, Bloomberg, WSJ, Barron's), Claude generates talking points from the headline and summary only, since the full article text is inaccessible. This is indicated by the `$` badge on the headline card.

Talking points are cached in `article_talking_points` after first generation, keyed by `SHA-256(url) + briefing_date`. Subsequent requests for the same article on the same day return from cache instantly.
